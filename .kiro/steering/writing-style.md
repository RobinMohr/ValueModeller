# Writing Style

When writing prose, documentation, or any non-code text in this project, follow these rules:

## Punctuation

- Never use em dashes (—). Use colons, periods, commas, or restructure the sentence instead.
- Avoid semicolons in casual/documentation writing. Just use two sentences.
- Use parentheses sparingly. If the aside is important, make it its own sentence.

## Sentence Structure

- Vary sentence length. Mix short punchy sentences with longer explanatory ones.
- Don't make every paragraph follow the same pattern. Avoid the "statement. Elaboration. Consequence." template that repeats identically across sections.
- Break perfect symmetry. If you have a list of three things, they don't all need to be the same grammatical structure.
- Start some sentences with "And" or "But" or "So" when it fits. Real people do this.

## Word Choice

- Don't use "crucial", "vital", "essential", "robust", "comprehensive", "innovative", "cutting-edge", "seamless", "leverage", "elevate", "streamline", or "paradigm" unless quoting someone.
- Prefer concrete words over abstract ones. "The build broke" over "a failure was encountered in the build process."
- Say "about" instead of "approximately". Say "use" instead of "utilise". Say "help" instead of "facilitate".

## Tone

- Write like you're explaining something to a colleague at a whiteboard, not presenting at a conference.
- It's fine to be informal. Contractions are good. Incomplete sentences are sometimes fine for emphasis.
- Don't hedge everything. If something is true, just say it. Drop "it's worth noting that" and "it should be mentioned that."
- Avoid the pattern of making a claim and then immediately restating it in fancier words.

## Structure

- Not everything needs a header. Sometimes a paragraph break is enough.
- Don't over-list. If you have 2 items, just write them in a sentence. Lists are for 4+ items or when scanability matters.
- Introductions don't need to summarise what's coming. Just start.
